# now-turbotaxfree

This repo is a plain-language note on TurboTax — what it is, how the "free" version actually works, and why so many people search for it as a single word.

## Why "turbotaxfree" is a thing

Typing `turbotaxfree` instead of `turbo tax free` is not a mistake. It's how people actually talk about the product out loud, and search engines have learned to map that mashed-together string back to the same software. The name "TurboTax" is two words with a capital in the middle, but in URLs, app stores, and casual chat it almost always appears as one. So a query like `turbotaxfree` is simply the brand name plus the word "free," squeezed together.

If you typed that, you were almost certainly looking for one of two things:

1. The free tier of TurboTax — the version that costs nothing to prepare and file a basic return.
2. A way to use TurboTax without paying, without being steered into an .

Both are legitimate, and both deserve a straight answer.

## What TurboTax actually is

TurboTax is a tax-prep software product made by Intuit. It has been around since the 1980s, originally as a floppy-disk program, and it is now one of the most widely used ways for individuals to prepare their own federal and state returns. It walks you through a series of questions about your income, deductions, and life events, then fills out the tax forms for you and lets you file electronically.

The product line is split into tiers:

- **TurboTax Free Edition** — for simple returns (W-2 income, standard deduction, no itemizing).
- **TurboTax Deluxe** — for homeowners, itemized deductions, and charitable contributions.
- **TurboTax Premier** — for investments, rental property, and capital gains.
- **TurboTax Self-Employed** — for freelancers, contractors, and small-business income.

The higher tiers cost money. The free edition is where most of the confusion lives.

## The fine print on "free"

Here is the honest version of how the free tier works, because the marketing language has caused a lot of frustration over the years.

**The good news:** If your situation is genuinely simple — you have a W-2, you take the standard deduction, you have no dependents claiming special credits, and you have no investment or self-employment income — the Free Edition will prepare and your federal return at no cost. State returns are usually extra, even on the free tier.

**The catch:** The software does not always know, at the start, whether you qualify. You begin the interview, answer a few questions, and then the program may tell you that your situation requires a paid version. This is not a , but it is a well-known source of frustration. Plenty of people have typed `turbotaxfree`, clicked into the product, and then been told they need to upgrade halfway through.

**The other catch:** Some years, Intuit has offered "free" versions that actually require you to use their own payment or refund-advance products to qualify. Those offers change from season to season, so the safest reading is: if a screen asks you to pay for a state return or an upgrade, you are no longer on a truly free path.

If your return is simple, the free tier works as advertised. If it is not, the honest answer is that you will either pay for TurboTax or use a different product.

## What people usually mean when they search "turbotaxfree"

The search variant tells you something about intent. A person who types the whole thing as one word is usually not a tax professional. They are someone who heard "TurboTax is free" and wants to confirm it before they start. They may also be someone who has used TurboTax before, hit a at the end of a session, and is now looking for the version that does not do that.

A few practical notes for that person:

- The free tier is a real product, not a trial. You do not need a credit card to start. You only pay if you choose a paid tier or a paid add-on like state filing.
- You can do the entire interview before paying anything. The product only asks for payment at the point of filing.
- If you are asked for payment early, it is because the software has decided your return needs a higher tier — not because the free version is broken.
- The "Free Edition" name has changed over the years. Some seasons it was called "TurboTax Free," some seasons "TurboTax Free Edition," and the exact qualification rules have shifted. Always check the current season's eligibility page rather than trusting a blog post from three years ago.

## Alternatives if the free tier does not fit

If TurboTax's free tier turns out not to cover your situation, you are not stuck. A few honest alternatives:

- **IRS Free File** — a partnership program where several tax software vendors (including TurboTax in some years) offer free preparation for taxpayers below an income threshold. Eligibility is income-based and changes yearly.
- **Free File Fillable Forms** — the IRS's own electronic versions of paper forms. No interview, no guidance, no math help — you fill in the fields yourself. It is free but requires you to know what you are doing.
- **Other commercial software** — H&R Block, TaxAct, and others all have free tiers with different qualification rules. If one product says you need to upgrade, another may cover your situation for free.
- **Volunteer Income Tax Assistance (VITA)** — a real, nationwide program where IRS-certified volunteers prepare basic returns for free for people who qualify by income or age. This is not software; it is a human being who does the work.

The honest summary: TurboTax's free tier is genuinely free for a narrow slice of taxpayers, and genuinely not free for everyone else. The search `turbotaxfree` is really asking "can I do this without paying?" — and the answer is "maybe, depending on your return." That is not a satisfying answer, but it is the true one.
